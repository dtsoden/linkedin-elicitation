# Reply mode

The comments are where an elicitation post pays off, both in reach and in what you learn. The user pastes comments from a live post. Sort each one, draft a reply where one is needed, and then update the research note.

## Sorting

| Kind | What to do |
|---|---|
| Correction, and they're right | Concede in the first sentence, thank them by name, and say what you'll change. No "great point, but." |
| Correction you disagree with | Say once, specifically, where you differ. If they come back with the same point, let them have the last word. |
| They shared their own number or practice | Ask one follow-up about how or why. This is the research. |
| Disagreement with the claim | Restate your reasoning in one or two sentences, ideally with a detail you left out of the post. |
| Says the post is bait, or a trick | Answer plainly and without defensiveness: why you believe the claim. Tell the user to record it with `--bait-callouts`. |
| Pitch, spam, or "great post!" | No reply needed. A reaction is enough for friendly ones. |
| Hostile or personal | No reply. Tell the user, and let them decide whether to hide or report it. |

## Writing replies

- Under 1,250 characters, and usually under 300. Address the commenter, not the audience.
- Use the commenter's first name once at most.
- No pitch, no link to your services, and no "DM me" in a reply to someone who taught you something.
- Every reply stays within `facts.md`, the same as the post. If a reply needs a number you don't have, leave it out.
- Give each reply its own fenced code block, labeled with the commenter's name outside the block.

## Research note

Keep one note per post, at `research/<post file name>.md`, using `templates/research-note.md`. Record what commenters shared: their numbers, the methods they named, the objections they raised. Quote them exactly and note who said what. When a pattern shows up in three or more posts, point it out to the user, because it's often their next post.

Never quote a commenter in a future post without asking them first.
